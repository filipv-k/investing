## Thesis
_Edited in place. When the argument itself changes, bump the version and note why in the Validation Log._

**Version:** v1 · **Last revised:** 2026-10-02 · **Overall conviction:** medium

**One-line thesis:**
> The "SaaSpocalypse" is not in Salesforce's numbers: core application revenue is growing 8%, bookings are the strongest in four years, management reports growing seats and near-record-low attrition, and Agentforce has reached $1.5B of recurring revenue. But growth without acquisitions is still only ~6–7%, the headline figures are flattered by Informatica, Anthropic gains and a debt-funded buyback, and the stock has recovered 58% from its June low. The thesis is confirming; the next proof is the organic re-acceleration promised for the second half. Hold; add near ~$200, trim near ~$300.

**Full argument:**
The idea: the market's fear that AI agents will replace the people who pay for software by the seat — the "SaaSpocalypse" — will not come true for Salesforce, and the company will succeed with its own agent product, Agentforce. The fear took the stock from $265 at the end of 2025 to $150 on 2026-06-22; I bought at $171.64 on 2026-07-17.

Two quarters of results since the sell-off began say the fear has not materialized. The line that holds the seat-based products (Sales, Service, Marketing, Commerce, Slack) grew 8% in constant currency in Q2 FY27, against 7% a quarter and a year earlier. Contracted revenue due within twelve months (cRPO) grew 14%, and total contracted revenue 11%. Management says seats in Sales, Service and Slack grew year on year, attrition is near its lowest level ever, contract lengths are increasing and net new order value is growing at the fastest rate in four years. The last four are statements without published figures, but they are consistent with the reported lines and with full-year guidance being raised at each report.

Agentforce is real and still small. Its recurring revenue went from about $0.44B a year ago to $0.8B in January and more than $1.5B in July, roughly 3.5% of subscription revenue; my estimate is that it supplied about a third of the growth that did not come from acquisitions. Usage is compounding (3.2B agent tasks in the quarter, +97% on the previous one), half of bookings are customers topping up consumption, and 2,000 paying customers went into production in the quarter. Two caveats: the Q2 figure was widened to include Slackbot and "Headless 360", and the headline "$3.9B, +210%" for AI and data includes about $1.1B that came with the Informatica acquisition.

The headline numbers are better than the underlying ones. Revenue grew 11%, of which 4.5 points are Informatica; without it, 6–7%. Reported EPS doubled, but $2.53 of the $5.90 is a gain on the Anthropic stake and the share count is down 14.7%; without the gain, non-GAAP EPS rose 16% and GAAP EPS fell 5%, because interest expense is now seven times higher and GAAP operating income was flat. Management has promised that growth without acquisitions re-accelerates in the second half and calls the Q3 part of that "math"; that is the next test.

Capital allocation has been bold. Salesforce borrowed $25B in March to buy back about 14% of its shares at an average of $176 so far — well below today's price — taking debt to $39.5B. In twelve months it also committed about $14.5B to acquisitions (Informatica, Qualified, Contentful, Fin). And it is tied to Anthropic three ways: as an investor (a $3.0B gain this year), as a partner ("Claudeforce", Claude working inside Salesforce data) and as a potential competitor, since Anthropic's product launches set off the sell-off in the first place.

At $236.69 the stock is at 17x this year's EPS without gains and a 7.7% free-cash-flow yield. That is no longer the 11x of June, but it still does not assume growth.

### Pillars
1. **The seat-based core holds** — AI agents add to, and do not replace, the paid users of Sales, Service and Slack.
   - *Confirms if:* core application revenue growth ≥7% in constant currency; management keeps reporting seat growth and low attrition; contract lengths stable
   - *Breaks if:* core application growth below 5% for two quarters; attrition rising; seat declines admitted or visible in shrinking cRPO
   - *Current status:* confirming — core applications +8% (7% a quarter and a year ago); seats up and attrition near a record low per management; premium-edition bookings doubled on the quarter. Caveat: seat and attrition data are not published as numbers

2. **Agentforce becomes a material growth engine** — agents are sold on top of seats and through consumption.
   - *Confirms if:* Agentforce ARR adding ≥$300M a quarter on a stable definition; usage and customers in production rising; consumption top-ups continuing
   - *Breaks if:* ARR additions below $200M for two quarters; another re-definition that hides a slowdown; pricing cuts to buy adoption
   - *Current status:* confirming — $0.8B → $1.2B → >$1.5B over three quarters; 2,000 customers added to production in Q2; bookings doubled YoY. Caveat: definition widened in Q2; Salesforce is also buying agent capability (Fin, $3.6B; Qualified, $1.2B)

3. **Growth without acquisitions re-accelerates** — from ~6–7% toward the ~10% the FY30 target of $63B+ requires.
   - *Confirms if:* organic growth above Q2's level in Q3 and Q4; cRPO growth ≥14%; FY28 guide consistent with ~11% a year to FY30
   - *Breaks if:* no acceleration in the second half; cRPO growth below 10%; FY30 target reached only by more acquisitions, or cut
   - *Current status:* neutral — bookings indicators are strong and the full-year guide was raised by $100M on organic performance, but reported organic growth has not yet moved (≈6% in Q4 FY26, ≈7–8% in Q1, ≈6–7% in Q2)

4. **Margins and cash hold while AI costs rise** — efficiency offsets compute and acquisition costs.
   - *Confirms if:* non-GAAP operating margin ≥34%; free cash flow growing; subscription gross margin stabilizing
   - *Breaks if:* non-GAAP margin below 33%; free cash flow down YoY; subscription gross margin falling more than 2 points a year
   - *Current status:* confirming, with pressure — non-GAAP margin 34.1% (34.3% a year ago), first-half free cash flow +11%; but subscription gross margin 81.3% vs. 83.0%, GAAP margin 20.5% vs. 22.8%, and the free-cash-flow growth guide was cut from 9–10% to 4–5% because of interest on the buyback debt

5. **Capital allocation adds value per share without over-stretching the balance sheet.**
   - *Confirms if:* buyback executed below intrinsic value; net debt falling from free cash flow; acquisitions small and integrated
   - *Breaks if:* another large debt-funded buyback or an acquisition above ~$10B; net debt above ~2.5x operating income; write-downs
   - *Current status:* confirming so far — about 14% of shares bought at ~$176 against a $237 price; net debt $28B, about 1.8x non-GAAP operating income; $11.3B of strategic investments on top. Caveats: $14.5B of acquisitions in a year, 190% of FY27 free cash flow returned, equity down from $59B to $38B

### Thesis test — is the "SaaSpocalypse" in the numbers?

| The fear | What would show it | What Q2 FY27 shows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Agents replace seats | Core application revenue shrinking | Core applications +8% in constant currency (7% a quarter and a year earlier); seats in Sales, Service and Slack up YoY (management) |
| Customers leave | Attrition rising | "Near its lowest level ever" (management; no figure) |
| Pricing power erodes | Shorter contracts, lower contracted revenue | Contract lengths increasing; total contracted revenue $66.3B (+11%); premium editions at $195–550 per user a month, bookings doubled on the quarter |
| Growth stalls | Bookings and backlog falling | cRPO +14% (≈10% without Informatica, same as a year ago); net new order value growth strongest in four years (management); FY27 guide raised at both reports this year |
| AI costs eat the margin | Gross margin falling | Partly visible: subscription gross margin 81.3% vs. 83.0%; non-GAAP operating margin 34.1% vs. 34.3% |

| Recurring revenue, $B | Q2 FY26 | Q3 FY26 | Q4 FY26 | Q1 FY27 | Q2 FY27 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Agentforce | ~0.44 | >0.5 | 0.8 | 1.2 | >1.5¹ |
| Agentforce + Data 360 | 1.2 | ~1.4 | >2.9² | ~3.4 | ~3.9 |

¹ Includes Slackbot and Headless 360 from Q2 FY27. ² Includes ~$1.1B Informatica Cloud ARR from Q4 FY26.

- **No SaaSpocalypse so far.** Every reported line that would show it is stable or better, two quarters into the sell-off. The strongest evidence is the core application line growing 8% and the guide being raised at each report.
- **Agentforce is succeeding, from a small base.** About 3.5% of subscription revenue and, by my estimate, roughly a third of organic growth. Without it the rest of the business would be growing about 4–5%.
- **What is organic and what is bought.** Revenue +11% is ≈+6–7% organic. Non-GAAP EPS +103% is +16% without the Anthropic gain, and most of that is the buyback: operating income grew 10%.
- **What is still unproven.** The organic re-acceleration; whether seats hold once agents do more of the work (seat data is not published); and whether margins hold as usage-based revenue replaces seat revenue.
- **Verdict:** the thesis is supported by the evidence available — more clearly on "no SaaSpocalypse" than on "Agentforce changes the growth rate". It has had two quarters to be wrong and has not been.

- **Cash flow:** FY27 free cash flow ≈$15.0B → **7.7% yield** (P/FCF 12.9x, EV/FCF 14.8x). After stock-based compensation ($3.6B a year) 5.9%. On the ~790M shares left after the buyback settles, about $19 a share, an 8% yield.
- **Sales and profit:** EV/Sales 4.8x FY27E; 14x non-GAAP operating income; 24x GAAP operating income.
- **Two reference values (judgment, not a model):**
  - *Thesis right* — no seat erosion, growth without acquisitions back toward ~10%: 18–22x → **$250–300 a share**.
  - *Thesis wrong* — seat compression arrives and growth falls to low single digits: 11–13x → **$150–180 a share**, i.e. the June low and my cost.
  - At $236.69 the market is about two-thirds of the way from the second to the first.
- **What has to be true for today's price:** the core keeps growing ~7–8%, margins hold at ~34%, and free cash flow stays near $15B. No acceleration is needed at this price; it is needed for $300.
- **Street:** 55 analysts, consensus Buy, average PT ~$281 (+19%), range $160–475. Morgan Stanley Equal-weight $235 ("Agentforce scaling is still early"); UBS Hold $260.
- **Positioning:** hold. Add near ~$200 (14.6x, the 200-day average) if pillars 1–2 hold. Trim near ~$300 unless organic growth has reached ~10%.

### Kill criteria (sell / abandon triggers)
- No organic re-acceleration in the second half of FY27 — growth without acquisitions at or below Q2's ~6–7% in both Q3 and Q4, after management called it "math" (pillar 3)
- Core application revenue growth below 5% in constant currency for two consecutive quarters, or attrition and seat declines acknowledged (pillar 1 — the SaaSpocalypse arriving)
- cRPO growth below 10% in constant currency including acquisitions
- Agentforce ARR additions below $200M for two consecutive quarters, or a further re-definition of the metric (pillar 2)
- Non-GAAP operating margin below 33%, or free cash flow declining YoY (pillar 4)
- Another large debt-funded buyback or an acquisition above ~$10B; net debt above ~2.5x non-GAAP operating income (pillar 5)
- FY30 revenue target of $63B+ cut, or kept only by adding acquisitions
- Valuation — trim, not exit: above ~$300 (≥22x EPS without gains) without organic growth near 10%
