## Thesis
_Edited in place. When the argument itself changes, bump the version and note why in the Validation Log._

**Version:** v1 · **Last revised:** 2026-10-02 · **Overall conviction:** low

**One-line thesis:**
> Owner-operators holding 32% fixed Groupon's cost base and stopped the cash burn, but the rest of the thesis — reliable execution and a new growth model — is not in the numbers: growth came back for one year on heavier marketing and has stalled again, with three straight top-line misses. At $19.53 the stock already prices a recovery that has to appear in Q3. Hold the small position, do not add; decide on the November print.

**Full argument:**
The idea I started from: the new owners and management — Pale Fire Capital, the Prague investment group whose co-founder Dusan Senkypl has run Groupon since March 2023 — would cut costs, fix execution and find a new growth model. Tested against the numbers through Q2 2026 (reported 2026-08-06), only the first of the three is done.

Costs. SG&A fell from $350M (2023) to $274M (2025) and headcount from 2,213 (end-2023) to 1,442 (June 2026); a business that burned about $170M of cash in 2022 produced $50M of free cash flow in 2025. A further restructuring (up to 400 positions, $20–25M of annual savings) is under way. But most of what was saved was re-spent: marketing rose from $111M to $166M (21% → 33% of revenue), so contribution profit — gross profit after marketing — fell from $340M to $287M. Adjusted EBITDA went from $55.5M to $69.3M and has been flat for two years. After $41.5M of stock-based compensation it is about $25M, and operating income is close to zero.

Execution and growth. 2025 was the first year of growth in a decade (billings +7%, revenue +1%), but it came with 15% more marketing, flat units (+1%) and lower contribution profit — bought, not earned. It did not last. North America Local billings growth went +20% → +18% → +9% → +2% → −1% over five quarters, the top line has landed below the company's own guidance three quarters in a row, and units fell 7% in Q2'26. Management kept its full-year guidance, which now needs revenue growth of +6–10% in the second half and at least $58M of free cash flow with Q3 guided negative; its own letter says the required acceleration "runs ahead of our current pace". The growth model on offer is an "AI-native" Groupon: Project Foundry (AI agents inside every function), deals bookable by AI assistants, personalization, a quality bar on deals. There are early signs — organic traffic growing again, July at mid-single-digit growth — but no revenue evidence yet.

Why this is a hold and not a sell. The people making the decisions own 32% of the company and have not sold; the company bought 2.8M shares at $11.21 when the market gave up in spring; the cash covers the $46M of notes due in March 2027; and with ~32% of the shares sold short, one quarter that simply meets guidance would move the price sharply. Why it is not a buy: at $19.53 the price already assumes the recovery (no-growth value is roughly $10–13 a share), my cost is $25.70, and the Q3 print decides which of the two it is.

### Pillars
1. **Cost discipline holds and turns into profit** — the cost base stays right-sized and savings reach the bottom line instead of being re-spent.
   - *Confirms if:* FY26 adjusted EBITDA ≥$75M; restructuring delivers $20–25M of annual savings; contribution profit stops falling
   - *Breaks if:* EBITDA guide cut; savings fully absorbed by marketing with no growth to show for it
   - *Current status:* confirming, but largely harvested — SG&A −$77M (−22%) from 2023 to 2025, headcount −35% since end-2023; adjusted EBITDA flat at $69.3M for two years and only $24.5M TTM after stock-based compensation

2. **Management delivers what it guides** — results land inside the guided ranges, top line first.
   - *Confirms if:* Q3 billings within +4–6%; platform migration and restructuring finished in Q3 as promised; Enterprise channel and new-merchant acquisition recover
   - *Breaks if:* a fourth straight top-line miss, or a full-year guide cut
   - *Current status:* contradicting — top line below guidance in Q4'25, Q1'26 and Q2'26 (profit and cash were met in Q2); North America Local decelerated five quarters in a row; the platform rebuild took three years; a new COO and marketplace head started in August 2026 to fix the North America supply engine

3. **A repeatable growth model emerges** — growth that does not have to be bought with marketing.
   - *Confirms if:* billings growth ≥5% for two consecutive quarters with contribution profit rising; North America units and active customers growing; measurable volume from AI-agent and organic channels
   - *Breaks if:* billings flat or down through H2'26; contribution profit still falling; active customers below 15.5M
   - *Current status:* contradicting / unproven — 2025's +7% billings came with +15% marketing, −4% contribution profit and +1% units; H1'26 billings −1%, units −6%, North America active customers down two quarters; long-term target of 20%+ billings growth vs. −1% now. Green shoots: organic back to growth, July mid-single-digit, International ex-Giftcloud +5%, AI Builder Pack

4. **Owners stay aligned and allocate capital well** — the reason to trust a turnaround that has not yet shown up in growth.
   - *Confirms if:* Pale Fire and the CEO hold or buy; buybacks made at low prices; no dilutive deals
   - *Breaks if:* open-market selling by Pale Fire or the CEO; CEO departure; equity issuance
   - *Current status:* confirming — Pale Fire 10.18M shares (25.0%) plus the CEO's 2.85M held directly (7.0%); the CEO exercised 3.06M options in June and kept the net shares; 2.8M shares bought back at $11.21 in H1'26, none since the price rose. Caveats: stock-based compensation is 8% of revenue, so buybacks only offset dilution (share count −0.4% in H1); the July 2025 note exchange swapped $150M of notes convertible at $30 for $224M convertible at $54 — $74M more debt to push out maturity and dilution; management is courting retail holders (X Spaces, an interview with a shareholder-blogger)

5. **Cash generation covers the debt without dilution** — self-funded through the 2027 maturity and beyond.
   - *Confirms if:* FY26 free cash flow ≥$60M; 2027 notes repaid from cash; year-end cash ≥~$200M
   - *Breaks if:* free-cash-flow guide cut; new equity or convertible issuance; cash below ~$150M after March 2027
   - *Current status:* neutral — free cash flow positive in 2024 ($40.6M) and 2025 ($49.9M) but only $29.9M TTM and $1.5M in H1'26; net debt $64M (notes $290M, cash $226M); $201M of the cash is owed to merchants and equity is negative (−$72M); the SumUp stake ($75M carrying value) is a reserve

- **Costs: validated.** SG&A down $77M in two years, headcount down a third, cash burn turned into cash generation. The 2026 restructuring adds another $20–25M a year.
- **But the savings funded marketing, not profit.** From 2023 to 2025 SG&A fell $77M while marketing rose $55M and contribution profit fell $53M. Net gain in adjusted EBITDA: $14M, then nothing for two years.
- **Execution: not validated.** Three consecutive quarters below own top-line guidance; the core North America Local business went from +20% to −1% in a year.
- **Growth model: not validated.** The one growth year was price and mix on flat units, with more marketing per dollar of revenue. Units are now falling 7% and the customer base is flat.
- **Verdict:** one leg of three. What remains is a bet that owners with a third of the company will find the growth they have not found in three and a half years.

### Valuation — what has to be true
_(as of 2026-10-01 close)_ Price $19.53; market cap ~$794M (40.7M shares); notes $290M principal less cash $226M = net debt $64M; enterprise value (EV) ~$858M.
- **On management's measure:** 13.0x TTM adjusted EBITDA ($66.1M); 10.7–11.4x the FY26 guide ($75–80M); 1.7x revenue.
- **On what is left for shareholders:** adjusted EBITDA less stock-based compensation is $24.5M TTM → 35x. Free cash flow $29.9M TTM → 3.8% yield, or 7.6% if the ≥$60M guide is met. GAAP earnings are negative.
- **Outside the EV figure:** on the plus side the SumUp stake (carried at $74.8M, ~$1.84 a share, unlisted) and $29.8M of restricted cash; on the minus side $201M of the cash is merchants' money in transit and equity is negative.
- **Two reference values (judgment, not a model):**
  - *No growth* — ~$75M EBITDA at 6–8x → **$10–13 a share**. Where the stock traded in February–April 2026 and where the company was a buyer.
  - *Growth restored* — high-single-digit growth and ~$95–100M EBITDA in 2027 at 10–12x → **$22–27 a share**. The 2026 high was $28.58; the stock was at $33–35 in mid-2025 when North America Local grew 20%.
  - At $19.53 the market is about 60% of the way from the first to the second.
- **What has to be true for today's price:** billings growth back to ≥5% by Q4 without contribution profit falling further; FY26 EBITDA ≥$75M and free cash flow ≥$60M; no new dilution.
- **Dilution to carry in the share count:** 5.15M unvested stock awards (12.7% of shares; most performance units vest only at price hurdles of $20–69); the 2027 notes convert at $30 (1.5M shares), the 2030 notes at $54.04 (4.5M shares).
- **Street:** three analysts — Northland Buy $30, Roth Buy $40, Goldman Sachs Sell $22 (raised from $13 on 2026-08-10). Consensus revenue $519M for 2026 and $560M for 2027 (+8%).
- **Positioning:** no adds until a quarter meets its own top-line guidance, or the price is back in the $10–13 zone. Trim above ~$28–30 unless billings are growing at high single digits.

### Kill criteria (sell / abandon triggers)
- Q3'26 billings growth below the +4% low end of guidance, or North America Local billings down again — the fourth straight top-line miss (pillar 2 broken)
- FY26 guidance cut on revenue (below $513M) or free cash flow (below $60M)
- Contribution profit still falling YoY in Q4'26 while marketing rises — growth is still being bought (pillar 3)
- Active customers below 15.5M, or North America active customers down four quarters in a row
- Pale Fire or the CEO sell shares in the open market, or the CEO leaves (pillar 4)
- New equity or convertible issuance, or cash below ~$150M after the March 2027 repayment (pillar 5)
- Valuation — trim, not exit: above ~$28–30 without high-single-digit billings growth
