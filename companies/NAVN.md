## Thesis
**Version:** v1 · **Last revised:** 2026-08-04 · **Overall conviction:** medium

**One-line thesis:**
> Category leader modernizing corporate travel + expense with an integrated AI/payments platform, compounding ~30–40% with newly positive non-GAAP profits — quality growth story, but near all-time highs and priced for flawless execution; accumulate on weakness, not at highs.

**Full argument:**
Navan (ex-TripActions, IPO Oct 30 2025 at $25) combines travel booking, corporate cards/payments, and expense automation in one platform, displacing legacy stacks (SAP Concur + separate expense tools + agency TMCs). Q1 FY27 (ended Apr 2026): revenue $220M +40% YoY, GBV a record $3.1B +50%, non-GAAP operating income $24M, non-GAAP net profit $22M, GAAP net loss narrowed to ~$21M. FY27 guidance raised to $907–913M revenue (+30%) with ~9% non-GAAP operating margin. Commercial momentum is broad: enterprise wins (Cummins, Enbridge, Evotec), LatAm entry via Smartrips acquisition (Brazil), European supplier integrations, Hilton hotel direct-connect, new Events platform, and an MCP/AI-agent layer. Business travel volumes are setting records (Navan's own H1'26 benchmark). Risks: still GAAP-unprofitable (TTM net loss ~$357M, heavy SBC), extreme post-IPO volatility ($8.11–$29.71 in 12 months), a securities class action re: IPO disclosures plus fiduciary-duty investigations, competition from Concur/Amex GBT and fintechs (Brex, Ramp, Expensify), and cyclicality of corporate travel (oil/geopolitics sensitivity).

### Pillars
1. **Durable 30%+ growth** — enterprise + international expansion keeps revenue growth ≥30% with GBV growing at least as fast.
   - *Confirms if:* quarterly revenue growth ≥30% YoY; GBV growth ≥ revenue growth; continued large-logo wins
   - *Breaks if:* growth <25% for two consecutive quarters without compensating margin upside
   - *Current status:* confirming (Q1 FY27: +40% rev, +50% GBV)

2. **Operating leverage to real profitability** — non-GAAP profits scale and GAAP loss trends to breakeven.
   - *Confirms if:* FY27 non-GAAP op income ≥$76M delivered; GAAP loss narrows each quarter; FCF positive
   - *Breaks if:* margin guidance cut, GAAP losses re-widen, or SBC keeps diluting >5%/yr with no GAAP path
   - *Current status:* confirming (Q1 non-GAAP op income $24M; guide raised)

3. **Platform moat holds vs. legacy and fintech attackers** — integrated travel+payments+expense and AI agent sustain take rate and win rates vs. Concur, Amex GBT, Brex/Ramp/Expensify.
   - *Confirms if:* take rate stable, enterprise wins continue, supplier direct-connects (Hilton) improve economics
   - *Breaks if:* pricing/take-rate compression, notable churn, or suppliers disintermediate
   - *Current status:* confirming (Hilton + Singapore Airlines direct connects, OpenTable, DataVisor partnerships)

4. **Business-travel cycle stays supportive** — corporate travel volumes keep growing above GDP.
   - *Confirms if:* GBV growth ≥25–30%; industry benchmarks keep setting records
   - *Breaks if:* macro shock (oil, recession, geopolitics) cuts corporate travel; GBV growth <15%
   - *Current status:* confirming (H1'26 record business-travel volumes)

### Securities class action — McCown v. Navan, Inc. (N.D. Cal., No. 26-cv-01550)
- **Filed:** 2026-02-23 · **Claims:** Securities Act §11 / §12(a)(2) / §15 — strict liability/negligence, **no fraud alleged**. Limited to shares traceable to the Oct 2025 IPO ($25)
- **Allegations:** prospectus omitted (a) 39% QoQ surge in S&M spend (~$95M for quarter ended 2025-10-31 — closed one day after listing), (b) CFO departure six weeks post-IPO
- **Damages mechanics:** §11 cap = purchase price ($25) minus value at suit (~$10–11) or sale price, traceable shares only. Price recovery above $25 shrinks holder claims toward zero — viable class is mostly sellers in the $8–15 trough. Defendants hold a "negative causation" defense (Dec 2025 drop attributable to Q3 FY26 miss, not the omission)
- **Expected financial impact:** typical settlement range for this profile ~$15–75M in 2–4 years, mostly D&O-insured; direct cash cost to Navan possibly only the retention (~$5–20M). Indirect: defense fees $10–30M (partly insured), higher D&O premiums, management distraction, mild overhang on future equity raises
- **Companion matters:** Grabar Law fiduciary-duty probe + Shareholders Foundation / Berger Montague investigations — derivative-type actions typically settle for governance changes + plaintiff fees, minimal cash
- **Materiality verdict:** vs. ~$7.4B mkt cap, positive FCF, and $76–80M guided non-GAAP op income, even bad-case ~$75M largely-insured settlement is low single-digit % of value — annoying, not thesis-breaking. Kill criterion triggers only if it *develops materially*: adverse ruling, amended complaint adding fraud/10b-5 claims, or executive departures tied to the case
- **Next signposts:** motion-to-dismiss ruling (est. late 2026 / 2027); any amended complaint; lead-plaintiff deadline passed 2026-04-24
