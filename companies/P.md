## Thesis
_Edited in place. When the argument itself changes, bump the version and note why in the Validation Log._

**Version:** v1 · **Last revised:** 2026-10-02 · **Overall conviction:** medium

**One-line thesis:**
> Everpure is earning far more because AI demand has made flash scarce: it raised prices about 150%, kept taking share, and operating profit is up 77%. The direct AI legs — hyperscaler deployments and the new data and AI products — are real but still small, and only start to show in revenue from the second half of FY27. The profit is confirmed; the "AI strategy" is not yet. After +91% the stock needs flash prices to stay high through FY28 while hyperscale ramps. Hold; no adds at the high, add near ~$100, trim above ~$155.

**Full argument:**
The idea I started from: Everpure profits from AI hardware demand and from its new AI strategy. The numbers through Q2 FY27 (reported 2026-08-26) and the analyst meeting (2026-09-23) confirm the profits and show that, so far, they come from a different mechanism than the label suggests.

What drives growth today is price. AI demand has made flash scarce: since Q3 FY26 the company's QLC flash costs are up 560% and the cost of a system up 153%. Everpure raised prices by about 150% on average and product revenue still grew 54%, which means it sold fewer systems — management says so ("customers are buying fewer solutions at higher prices"). The whole industry is doing the same: NetApp's product revenue grew 51% in the same quarter. What is specific to Everpure is the evidence of share gain underneath: subscription contract value signed +121%, RPO +44%, international revenue +75%, deals above $20M up nearly fivefold, and growth of 65% once subscription deals are counted like product sales.

The direct AI legs are still ahead. Hyperscale — Everpure's flash software and modules inside the storage of two of the five largest cloud companies — contributed "only minimally" through Q2; management expects significant revenue in Q3 and Q4 at 75–85% gross margin, with commitments into calendar 2028 for tens of exabytes and the second customer ramping from FY28. The new AI and data products (FlashBlade//EXA for GPU clouds, Data Stream, Data Intelligence from the $126M 1touch acquisition) have first sales and good benchmarks, but management's own plan has all new markets together at about 20% of revenue only by FY30. The core storage business stays ~80%.

Profit quality is mixed. Non-GAAP operating margin rose to 19.4% from 15.1% because costs grew 25% against revenue growth of 38%. But gross margin fell 2.2 points (the company chose not to pass on all of the cost increase), stock-based compensation is 13.5% of revenue so the GAAP margin is 5.3%, and first-half free cash flow was −$126M because Everpure pre-bought $654M of components and committed to a further $1.1B through 2027. The full-year cash-flow guide of $600–800M needs $726–926M in the second half.

The outlook is strong and cyclical. Management's preliminary FY28 view is revenue of $7.0–7.3B (+39–45%) and operating income of $1.7–1.9B (a 24–26% margin). It assumes flash prices stay near current levels. In the last flash glut (FY24) Everpure's revenue grew 3%. Management's answer is that it gained share in both rising and falling price cycles and that hyperscale will carry growth; that is the bet.

The stock has run ahead: $56.99 in March, my purchase at $70.09 in July, $134.13 now — helped by the second hyperscaler win, S&P 500 inclusion and the FY28 outlook. At ~26–32x the FY28 outlook it prices that outlook as delivered.

### Pillars
1. **Pricing power and share gains in core storage** — Everpure passes through flash inflation and keeps winning customers; this is today's profit engine.
   - *Confirms if:* revenue growth at or above guidance; product gross margin ≥65%; subscription contract value and RPO growing faster than revenue
   - *Breaks if:* product gross margin below 65% for two quarters; growth below guide; RPO growth slowing sharply (demand was pulled forward or destroyed by price)
   - *Current status:* confirming — revenue +38%, full-year guide raised twice ($4.35B → $4.46B → $5.05B midpoint). Caveat: growth is price-led on lower unit volumes, and NetApp's product revenue is growing almost as fast (+51% vs. +54%)

2. **Hyperscale becomes a real business** — the direct link to AI infrastructure spending.
   - *Confirms if:* "significant" hyperscale revenue in Q3 and Q4 FY27 visible in product gross margin; second customer ramps in FY28; a third design win
   - *Breaks if:* the ramp slips; a customer cuts or delays commitments; revenue stays immaterial through FY27
   - *Current status:* neutral, leaning confirming — two top-five design wins, firm commitments into calendar 2028, but revenue "minimal" so far; only two customers and lumpy; the largest prize (replacing hard drives) waits for flash prices to normalize

3. **The AI and data strategy adds new revenue** — Scale AI (FlashBlade//EXA) and Modern Data Software (Data Intelligence, Data Stream, Portworx).
   - *Confirms if:* disclosed new-market revenue on track to ~20% of the total by FY30; //EXA wins at GPU clouds; Data Intelligence attaching to the installed base
   - *Breaks if:* no measurable revenue by the FY28 report; losses to VAST, DDN or WEKA in large GPU clusters; 1touch write-down
   - *Current status:* unproven — first Data Stream sales, //EXA customer wins, top MLPerf storage results, "steady increase in GPU attachments"; disclosed only once a year, and software is the area where Everpure is least differentiated

4. **The subscription engine gives durability through the cycle** — Evergreen//One and renewals keep growing when product pricing turns.
   - *Confirms if:* ARR growth ≥20%; RPO growth ≥25%; subscription gross margin ~75%
   - *Breaks if:* ARR growth below 15%; subscription margin falling as flash costs hit the as-a-service fleet
   - *Current status:* confirming — ARR $2.13B (+20%), RPO $4.09B (+44%), subscription contract value on a $1B+ annual run rate (FY26: $520M); subscription gross margin 74.9% (76.5% a year ago)

5. **Growth turns into cash and survives the flash cycle** — margins expand, pre-buys unwind, and the business holds up when prices fall.
   - *Confirms if:* FY27 free cash flow within $600–800M; operating margin moving toward 24–26% in FY28; product gross margin back toward 70% as costs stabilize; dilution ≤~2% a year
   - *Breaks if:* cash-flow guide missed or another large pre-buy; FY28 outlook cut; flash prices fall and revenue follows, as in FY24 (+3%)
   - *Current status:* neutral — margin and outlook strong, but first-half free cash flow −$126M, gross margin down 2.2 pts, stock-based compensation 13% of revenue (diluted shares +2.3% YoY despite $326M spent on buybacks and tax withholding in H1), and the FY28 outlook rests on flash prices staying high

- **Profits from AI hardware demand: confirmed, indirectly.** Operating income is growing 50% this year and is guided to roughly double next year. The cause so far is the flash shortage that AI created: prices at a ten-year high, passed through by a vendor with pricing power. Unit volumes are down.
- **The direct AI revenue is still to come.** Hyperscale was "minimal" through Q2 and ramps from Q3; the second customer from FY28. This is the part that would outlast the price cycle.
- **The new AI strategy cannot be validated yet.** First sales only; one disclosure a year; ~20% of revenue by FY30 on management's own plan.
- **The cycle is visible in the company's own history.** FY24, the last flash glut, was a +3% year. The FY28 outlook assumes no such turn.
- **Verdict:** the profit half of the thesis is validated and is stronger than expected; the AI-strategy half is a promise with a credible first instalment (two hyperscalers). The risk is that the market is capitalizing shortage pricing as if it were permanent.

- **What has to be true for today's price:** flash prices stay near current levels through FY28; hyperscale revenue arrives on schedule at 75–85% gross margin; the pre-buys turn back into cash; operating margin reaches the mid-20s.

Add near ~$100 (19–24x the FY28 outlook) if the pillars hold — post-earnings drops have offered that three times this year. ### Kill criteria (sell / abandon triggers)
- Product gross margin below 65% for two consecutive quarters — pricing power lost (pillar 1)
- FY27 revenue guide cut, or formal FY28 guidance below the preliminary $7.0B / $1.7B
- Hyperscale revenue still not "significant" by the Q4 FY27 report, or a hyperscale commitment cut or delayed (pillar 2)
- Flash contract prices falling two quarters in a row while Everpure's RPO growth drops below 25% — the cycle turning without the subscription cushion
- FY27 free cash flow below $600M, or another quarter of large negative cash flow from pre-buys (pillar 5)
- ARR growth below 15% (pillar 4)
- Valuation — trim, not exit: above ~$155 (≥30x the FY28 outlook before tax, ≥37x after) without the outlook being raised
