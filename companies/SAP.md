## Thesis

_Edited in place. When the argument itself changes, bump the version and note why in the Validation Log._

**Version:** v2 (SHORT case) · **Last revised:** 2026-08-19 · **Overall conviction:** medium

**One-line thesis:**

> The market is pricing SAP as an AI winner (bull case per Q2'26 call: AI drives top and bottom line), but underneath the headline cloud number the acquired portfolio is churning, R&D capability is degraded by a proprietary legacy stack + talent gap + investor cost pressure, and AI-native ERP challengers are emerging — SAP loses the AI race to MS/Google while its non-core revenues erode.

**Full argument:**
SAP's growth story rests almost entirely on Cloud ERP Suite conversions of its captive install base, dressed up with an AI narrative. Three forces work against it: (1) the LOB portfolio bolted on through acquisitions (Concur, Signavio, Ariba, Fieldglass, Qualtrics-era leftovers) is losing to best-of-breed and AI-native rivals, and SAP's bundled "Cloud ERP Suite" reporting hides this churn; (2) SAP cannot win the AI platform race — its proprietary, dated tech stack (ABAP-centric, closed ecosystem) repels the AI talent it needs, while investor-driven cost discipline (12-month headcount freeze, "30% AI productivity" claims used to justify not hiring) starves R&D precisely when MS Copilot/Fabric and Google are outspending and out-shipping it; agentic AI also threatens to disintermediate the application layer SAP monetizes; (3) a new generation of AI-native ERP companies (Campfire et al.) is forming below SAP, attacking mid-market first — the same pattern by which SaaS ate on-prem. When cloud growth decelerates (management already guided CCB slowdown into YE'26) and AI monetization fails to show up in numbers, the AI premium in the multiple compresses.

### Pillars

1. **Portfolio churn** — the acquired non-core products (Concur, Signavio, Ariba, Fieldglass, etc.) are structurally losing share and eroding revenue, masked by bundle reporting.
   
   - *Confirms if:* non-Cloud-ERP-Suite cloud growth stays in low single digits or turns negative; SAP further reduces per-product disclosure; visible customer defections to Coupa/Workday/ServiceNow/AI-native tools; Cloud ERP Suite growth converges down toward total cloud growth as S/4 conversions saturate.
   - *Breaks if:* LOB lines re-accelerate, or bundle cross-sell demonstrably lifts net revenue retention (SAP citing rising NRR with numbers).
   - *Current status:* Q2'26 implied math — total cloud +24% cc, Cloud ERP Suite +27% at 88% of cloud ⇒ the residual ~12% of cloud grew only ~6% cc. Caveat: SAP counts Ariba/Fieldglass/Concur *inside* the Suite metric, so churn there is masked by S/4 strength within the bundle, not visible in the residual. Disclosure opacity is itself part of the thesis. **Status: supportive but not yet provable from public data.**

2. **Degraded R&D → loses the AI race** — proprietary outdated stack, missing AI talent, and investor cost pressure mean SAP cannot compete with MS, Google, and frontier-model ecosystems on agentic AI.
   
   - *Confirms if:* agent roadmap slips (Q3'26: Business AI Platform, Joule Work, ~50 assistants; YE'26: 400+ agents) or ships hollow; no separately disclosed AI revenue by FY27; senior AI/engineering departures; R&D intensity falls while hyperscalers ship enterprise agents that bypass SAP's application layer.
   - *Breaks if:* SAP delivers the roadmap on time with evidenced customer adoption and *paid* AI revenue; outcome-based pricing gains traction; partnerships effectively rent frontier capability (Asam explicitly avoiding "lock-in to any generic frontier model").
   - *Current status:* Q2 call claims AI in >90% of top-50 deals but discloses zero AI revenue; headcount freeze + "30% productivity" narrative consistent with cost pressure over capability building; R&D costs +14% on only +3% headcount (AI compute, not talent). **New evidence (2026-08): the April 2026 API policy blocking third-party AI access (see Legal §2) is defensive conduct — you block data when you can't win on product. Status: consistent with thesis; roadmap execution Q3–Q4'26 is the near-term test.**

3. **AI-native ERP challengers** — a new generation (Campfire, other AI-native entrants) raises competition from below, compressing pricing and stealing the next cohort of customers.
   
   - *Confirms if:* challenger ARR/funding momentum continues; SAP mid-market losses or discounting in RISE deals; ECC laggards choose third-party support + AI-native point tools over S/4 migration, shrinking the conversion funnel behind CCB.
   - *Breaks if:* challengers stall at enterprise-grade compliance/scale (the historical ERP moat); SAP's switching costs keep the install base captive through the migration cycle.
   - *Current status:* early-stage — challengers are mid-market, no visible enterprise displacement yet. The April 2026 API policy (Legal §2) accelerates this pillar: every toll gate and audit letter strengthens the composable-ERP movement (SAP as transactional backend, AI orchestration vendor-neutral via MCP). **Status: watchlist pillar, longest fuse of the three.**

### Valuation — what has to be true

At ~€180 (P/E ~26x TTM) the market pays an AI-winner premium on ~24% cloud growth. Short works if: cloud growth decelerates below ~20% cc and CCB growth slows toward high-teens (management *already* guided slight CCB deceleration into YE'26), AI monetization stays invisible, and the multiple compresses toward 18–20x on flattening estimates → meaningful downside from here. Honest counterweights: stock is already −25% Y/Y so part of the bear case is priced in; the +28% post-Q2 rebound gives entry but also shows squeeze risk on AI headlines; FCF (€3bn/quarter) and the captive maintenance base put a floor under the stock. Timing matters more than direction — the thesis needs the Q3'26–FY27 window where AI promises must convert to numbers.

#### ECC → S/4 Private conversion economics (the "resell" decomposition, est. 2026-08-19)

Per €100 of annual ECC maintenance (like-for-like unit):

- **Before:** €100/yr maintenance @ ~90% GM → ~€92 gross profit. Customer's own infra/basis ops → SAP gets €0.
- **After (RISE private):** subscription ~€200–220/yr at signing (SAP-cited 2x–3x conversion multiple, private cloud at low end; before typical 15–35% negotiated discounts). Decomposition:
  - Software subscription (S/4 + embedded support equivalent): ~55–65% (~€110–140) — mostly the old maintenance stream relabeled; true software uplift ~1.1–1.3x
  - Infrastructure (hyperscaler resale, 20–35% markup over list): ~25–30% (~€50–65) — pure passthrough
  - Embedded managed services (patching, backups, basis ops): ~10–15% (~€20–30) — previously done by customer IT/partners
- **Conclusion:** ~65–80% of the revenue *increment* on the same installation is infra resale + services SAP never used to book. Genuine new software value ≈ +10–30%. Margin: contract GM ~60–65% vs ~90% on replaced maintenance → **+110% revenue but only ~+40% gross profit** per conversion — explains cloud GM dilution (74.6%, falling).
- **Thesis implications:** supports the revenue-quality critique — a meaningful slice of +24% cloud / +26% CCB growth is mechanical relabeling of the €10bn maintenance base (>50% still on ECC) plus low-margin passthrough; the cited "4x–5x over 5 years" expansion depends on cross-sell/AI upsell that pillars 1–2 dispute. **BUT** it also cuts against timing: the untapped ECC base gives SAP several years of near-guaranteed optical cloud growth even with zero innovation — duration risk for the short.
- **Watch:** cloud GM trend (dilution = private-cloud/resell mix growing), support revenue decline rate vs cloud adds, any disclosure of conversion multiples in CMD/calls.

**Addendum (2026-08-19): RISE ≠ another boost — no double counting, finite fuel tank, bundle effects**

- **The 1→2 ratio IS the RISE program**, not something on top of it — RISE is the commercial packaging through which the conversion uplift is harvested. SAP's +24% cloud growth already contains this effect; never count "RISE growth" and "conversion uplift" separately.
- **Net group uplift is +1x, not +2x:** the old €100 maintenance leaves the support line and reappears inside the €200 cloud subscription (cloud line +2, support line −1). Hence structurally declining support revenue and flattering cloud-line optics.
- **At signing the boost hits CCB, not revenue:** 3–5yr contracts, revenue ratable (often year-1 ramp discount). Each conversion mechanically ~doubles that customer's backlog contribution — a big part of why CCB grows +26%.
- **Genuinely additive on top of 2x:** contract escalators (~3–5%/yr indexation), FUE/user growth, BTP consumption overage, cross-sell toward the claimed 4x–5x, AI monetization, Digital Access/API tolls. Everything except escalators is what Pillars 1–2 dispute.
- **Bundle effects (Signavio, IAG, BTP credits, Business Network, Build Work Zone ship inside RISE):**
  - Cross-sell partly *pre-spent*: these products are already inside the 2x at signing → upsell headroom to 4x–5x smaller than advertised; expansion depends on consumption overage + AI.
  - Adoption optics bundle-driven: "Signavio in every deal" / "AI+BDC in >90% of top-50 deals" is what default bundling produces. Bundled ≠ used; SAP discloses no activation/usage/standalone-renewal metrics.
  - IFRS 15 allocation recycles bundled-product revenue into "Cloud ERP Suite" (+27%) regardless of usage — standalone LOB churn shows up as mix, not as churn (masks Pillar 1).
  - Legal overlap: free/near-free Signavio bundling in RISE is the surviving predatory-pricing/bundling count in *Celonis* — forced unbundling or price transparency would degrade Suite growth optics (see Legal §1).
- **Finite fuel tank:** the 2x is a one-time per-customer step-up. ~€5–6bn maintenance still on ECC → converts to roughly €11–13bn cloud over the migration wave = several years of near-guaranteed mechanical cloud growth (duration risk for the short), after which (~2028+) growth must come entirely from the upsell/AI layer the thesis disputes.

### Kill criteria (sell / abandon triggers)

- CCB growth re-accelerates ≥26% cc for two consecutive quarters (backlog contradicts the erosion story).
- SAP discloses material, growing AI-attributable revenue with credible metrics.
- Non-Suite cloud lines re-accelerate to double digits, or SAP proves rising NRR on bundles.
- Agent roadmap delivered on schedule (Q3 platform + YE 400 agents) *with* independent evidence of customer adoption.
- Consensus estimates start rising again while the multiple holds — the compression window has closed.

## Legal & Regulatory

### 1. Celonis v. SAP — antitrust, N.D. Cal. (added 2026-08-19)

_Source: own deep-dive in `SAP_resources/SAP Lawsuit Win Likelihood_.txt` (spring 2025) + web update to Aug 2026._

**The case:** Celonis (process mining leader) sued SAP in March 2025 (3:25-cv-02519, Judge Chhabria, San Francisco) alleging SAP leverages ERP dominance to monopolize process mining after the 2021 Signavio acquisition: restricting third-party data access (Clean Core policy, killing RFC ABAP/ODP extraction, deprecating OData, pushing costly Datasphere), bundling Signavio free/below cost into RISE, spreading FUD about Celonis license compliance, coercing customers during S/4 migrations ("migration hostage"), and breaking open-ecosystem assurances made to regulators during the Signavio review.

**Status (Aug 2026):**

- Jun 2025: SAP **stipulated not to interfere** with the Celonis extractor / customer data access pending resolution — defused the preliminary injunction but already constrains the lock-in playbook.
- 2025 motions practice: core claims **survived** — monopolization ("SAP ERP data access market"), attempted monopolization (process mining), bundling/predatory pricing, false advertising; tying and promissory estoppel dismissed with leave to amend. Full discovery opened (SAP internals on APIs, pricing, partner programs, RISE contracts).
- May 2026: Celonis moved to file a 117-page Second Amended Complaint adding customer-harm and broken-regulatory-promises detail.
- **Trial: December 7, 2026** — near-term catalyst inside the short window; settlement conferences ongoing.

**Precedent — Teradata v. SAP (settled):** the older sister case (S/4HANA↔HANA tying + trade secret theft; 9th Cir. revived it Dec 2024 and blessed plaintiff's market-power economics) was **settled for $480M in Feb/Mar 2026, days before trial**. Read-through: SAP paid heavily to avoid a jury on the same "leverage ERP dominance" theory; validates the playbook narrative and sets a settlement anchor for Celonis.

**Why it matters for the thesis:**

- **Bigger than the damages number.** Celonis attacks the machinery of the current growth engine — RISE bundling, Clean Core, data-access control — the same lock-in that the conversion economics (see Valuation) depend on. A remedy/settlement forcing open, free data access weakens both the moat and the resale margin story; treble damages possible on antitrust counts.
- **Feeds Pillar 1:** the predatory-pricing claim implies Signavio only competes when given away — evidence of acquired-portfolio weakness, from SAP's own conduct.
- **Feeds Pillar 2 narrative:** SAP defending closed-stack behavior in court while claiming AI openness; discovery documents could produce ugly headlines through H2'26.
- **Watch:** ruling on the SAC, any settlement before Dec 7 trial (size vs. $480M anchor), whether remedies touch data-access pricing/Clean Core, EU/regulator follow-on interest.

### 2. April 2026 API Policy — SAP blocking third-party AI access to SAP data (added 2026-08-19)

_Source: `SAP_resources/e3mag-com-en-sap-erp-exit-with-model-context-protocol.md` (E3 Magazine, 2026-08-13) + web update._

**What SAP did:** New API Policy (April 2026). Section 2.2.2 prohibits using SAP APIs for "interaction or integration with (semi-)autonomous or generative AI systems" unless via an SAP-approved/certified path; bans systematic bulk extraction to external warehouses/lakes (Microsoft Fabric explicitly in scope); ODP-RFC already classified "prohibited use" for third parties (since Feb 2024). Enforcement lever: automated audits/metering + Digital Access "indirect use" fees. Official justification: system stability and cybersecurity ("safeguard solution health"). Effect: to feed S/4 data into external AI (Copilot Studio, Snowflake Cortex, any agent), customers are pushed into licensing BTP + Business Data Cloud — SAP as exclusive, toll-charging gatekeeper of the customer's own data.

**Counterforces already visible:** MCP ("USB-C for AI") makes circumvention technically easy — OData wrapped as MCP tools bypasses Joule/BTP entirely; a partner/open-source ecosystem is forming (SUSE Trento MCP, Theobald, open-source ABAP-MCP servers). Legally, though, an autonomous third-party agent hitting SAP via a customer's own MCP gateway still triggers the AI clauses — technical lock-in broken, contractual lock-in remains (audit/back-payment risk). DSAG (German user group) publicly critical: unclear contracts, opaque future pricing, "unacceptable."

**Chances SAP succeeds — my assessment:**

- **Near term (1–2 yrs): partially, yes.** Against its own customers SAP holds contract law + audit leverage, and history says customers settle rather than fight (indirect-access era, Diageo). Most enterprises mid-S/4-migration won't risk noncompliance → the policy channels demand into BTP/BDC and supports cloud numbers through the guidance window.
- **Medium term: probably not sustainable.** (a) **Antitrust:** this is precisely the conduct on trial in *Celonis* on Dec 7, 2026 — tightening restrictions mid-litigation is bold; a loss or remedy-bearing settlement could gut §2.2.2, and the $480M Teradata payout shows juries are a live threat to the playbook. Hyperscaler-aligned vendors (MS Fabric is explicitly targeted) are potential new plaintiffs/complainants with deep pockets. (b) **Regulatory:** EU data-portability ethos (Data Act), DSAG pressure, possible Commission/Bundeskartellamt interest in "pay to access your own data." (c) **Technical:** MCP enforcement is whack-a-mole; policing depends on audits that further alienate customers.
- Net: SAP extracts tolls and friction for a while, but the blocking strategy is more likely to end via court/regulator/customer revolt than to become a durable moat.

**Implications for the thesis:**

- **Confirms Pillar 2 (defensive posture):** you block data when you can't win on product. If Joule/BDC were winning on merit, an open ecosystem would be to SAP's advantage. The policy is itself evidence SAP fears losing the AI race to MS/Google at the data layer.
- **Accelerates Pillar 3:** every audit letter and toll gate strengthens the composable-ERP movement — SAP degraded to transactional backend, AI orchestration vendor-neutral via MCP — and improves the pitch of AI-native challengers.
- **Cuts both ways short-term:** BTP/BDC toll revenue supports cloud growth optics for a few quarters (against the short), but it's low-quality, coerced revenue — same quality problem as the resell economics in Valuation.
- **Legal convergence risk:** Celonis remedies + this policy = one court decision away from SAP losing data-access pricing power across the board.
- **Watch:** enforcement cases/audit disputes going public, DSAG escalation, EU complaint filings, hyperscaler responses (MS/Snowflake official workarounds or complaints), whether SAP softens §2.2.2 before the Celonis trial (a softening = tacit admission of legal risk), MCP gateway scope in SAP Integration Suite (Joule-only vs open).

### 3. o9 Solutions v. SAP — trade secrets, N.D. Tex. (added 2026-09-02)

_Source: full analysis in `SAP_resources/o9-vs-sap-litigation-analysis.md` (2026-09-02)._

**The case:** o9 Solutions (Dallas, AI supply-chain planning, ~$3.7bn valuation) sued SAP SE, SAP America and three ex-o9 executives on 2025-11-25 (3:25-cv-03245, Judge Lindsay / Magistrate Horan, jury demanded). Claims: DTSA, Texas UTSA, breach of contract, tortious interference. Allegation: the three bulk-downloaded **>22,000 files** before defecting — architecture of o9's Digital Brain / Knowledge Graph, roadmap, pricing, account intelligence — and SAP used them in **Integrated Business Planning** and its SCM planning roadmap. The defectors now hold senior SAP roles: **Stephan de Barse** (ex-CRO → President, Global Business Suite), **Stijn-Pieter van Houten** (→ CPO, SCM Planning), **Sean Zonneveld** (→ Global CRO, Procurement). No damages figure pleaded.

**Status (Sep 2026):**

- **2026-08-11:** Magistrate Horan recommended **denying all defense motions** — 12(b)(6) group-pleading, more definite statement, and the individuals' *forum non conveniens* bid to move the case to the Netherlands. Pleading survives as "minimally adequate"; Netherlands adequate but not "significantly more convenient," no European witnesses identified. The forum loss is the bigger one: it keeps SAP in front of a Dallas jury with punitive damages and US discovery live.
- Pleadings stage only: **no answer, no scheduling order, no trial date.** R&R awaiting Lindsay's de novo review of objections.
- **o9 has never moved for a preliminary injunction** — a plaintiff facing genuine irreparable product harm usually does. Point in SAP's favour.
- **SAP has taken no provision and does not name o9** in the H1 2026 report (Teradata was disclosed and provisioned) ⇒ under IAS 37 management does not yet see a probable, estimable loss.

**Expected timeline:** R&R adoption Q3–Q4'26 → answer + scheduling order around year-end → 2027 lost to trade-secret identification and cross-border discovery (German entity, Dutch defendants, Hague/GDPR friction) → depositions and peak settlement pressure H1'28 → SJ/*Daubert* late '28 → trial 2029–30 if it survives. **Base case: settlement in 2028.** Teradata ran 7.5 years and settled on the courthouse steps; ~15–20% odds this reaches a verdict.

**Damage estimate (my judgement, not sourced):** base case **$200–600m** (~40%), defense win/de minimis ~20%, nuisance settlement $50–150m ~20%, $600m–1.5bn ~15%, >$1.5bn tail with 2× exemplary ~5%. Anchor: **Teradata $480M**. Against ~€3bn quarterly FCF and a ~$252bn market cap that is one-time noise, and headline trade-secret verdicts survive appeal poorly (Appian $2.04bn vacated; Title Source $706m reversed) — which is why both sides settle at a discount to the tail.

**Why it matters for the thesis — modest, and honestly not a driver:**

- **Weak support for Pillar 2.** SAP recruited the commercial and product leadership of an AI-native planning challenger rather than building the capability — the same "buy/defend, don't out-build" pattern as Signavio and the API policy. But hiring competitors' executives is normal industry conduct; this is a data point, not evidence.
- **The money is immaterial to the short.** ~0.1–0.25% of market cap in the base case, absorbable as a one-off exactly like Teradata. Do not carry it in the valuation.
- **Two things could make it matter:** (a) an injunction or discovery finding touching the **IBP roadmap during the APO-sunset (2027) migration window** — o9, Kinaxis and Blue Yonder will already be using the complaint in planning bake-offs, and procurement committees are IP-sensitive mid-migration; (b) a personnel consequence for **de Barse**, a division president, which would be a visible governance event against the "Autonomous Enterprise" leadership narrative. Both currently low-probability.
- **Pattern risk:** Teradata → Celonis → o9 in sequence invites a "serial IP defendant" framing. Narrative only, unless discovery surfaces a bad internal document.
- **Watch:** Lindsay's ruling on the objections; the trade-secret identification order (narrowing to a handful of specific technical secrets compresses exposure sharply); **first appearance of o9 in SAP's contingent-liability note** (= management now sees probable loss); any belated PI motion; SAP counterclaims or a German/Dutch countersuit; departure or reassignment of any of the three.
