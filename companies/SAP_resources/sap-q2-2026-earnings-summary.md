# SAP Q2 2026 Earnings Call — Summary & Analysis

**Call date:** July 23, 2026 · **Speakers:** CEO Christian Klein, CFO Dominik Asam
**Focus:** growth drivers, timelines, cost control

## Growth drivers

**1. Cloud ERP Suite is the core engine.** Growing 27% and now 88% of all cloud revenue, this is where nearly all momentum sits. CCB acceleration to 26% (from Q1's pace) is the strongest forward indicator on the call — though management guided to a *slight deceleration* in CCB growth exiting the year, and the Reltio acquisition contributed less than 1pp of that growth. Legacy license revenue continues its structural decline (−32%), so the cloud transition is doing all the work.

**2. AI as the deal-driver, not yet the revenue line.** AI and Business Data Cloud featured in over 90% of the 50 largest Q2 deals — AI is winning deals rather than being sold standalone. Klein flagged an opportunity to "reset price levels" via outcome-based AI pricing versus legacy per-seat SaaS. Customer proof points cited: Amadeus's agent autonomously cleared ~40,000 incorrect payment transactions; one customer cut BI build time ~75%; Dexco eliminated 97% of legacy customizations and closed books 75% faster.

**3. Agentic roadmap with concrete timeline:**

- **Q3 2026:** Business AI Platform launch, Joule Work UI (already tested by ~4,000 internal SAP users), ~50 assistants, plus 3 ERP migration assistants built on 10 agents
- **Year-end 2026:** 400+ Autonomous Suite agents
- **Ongoing ambition:** compress new agent delivery to under three weeks
- Beta programs for the new platform were "immediately oversubscribed"; the Autonomous Enterprise launch at Sapphire drew record attendance and strong pipeline

**4. RISE migrations as the funnel.** Customers report up to 30% lower ERP migration costs, and migration assistants (Q3) are aimed squarely at accelerating the ECC→cloud conversion base — the multi-year pipeline behind CCB.

## Cost control

**Headcount freeze.** Klein was explicit: no further headcount increase over the next 12 months. R&D headcount grew only 3% YoY while R&D costs rose 14% — worth watching, though management attributes rising cost to AI compute, not people.

**AI-driven productivity as the margin lever.** Development teams claim ~30% average productivity gains from AI tooling; SAP targets training 90%+ of employees over the coming months. AI token consumption is now charged back to consuming functions with "granular" optimization — an early sign of discipline on inference costs.

**Expense ratio commitment.** The 80–90% expense-to-revenue framework is maintained even with recent M&A, per Asam.

**Margin pressure is M&A- and investment-driven, not operational.** Cloud gross margin dipped 0.7pp on one-time test-environment investments. Dremio and Prior Labs will dilute H2 operating profit by a low triple-digit €m; Reltio cost €10m (IFRS) in Q2. Asam framed M&A as buying data/ontology capability, "not to acquire growth."

## Guidance

Top line and FCF guidance **maintained**; non-IFRS operating profit trimmed by €0.1bn to **€11.2–11.8bn** solely for acquisition dilution. Macro caveats: general uncertainty, Middle East specifically noted. Asam: "the underlying trajectory of our business remains fully intact."

## Take

The quarter's tension: strong cloud/CCB momentum and heavy AI narrative vs. a small profit-guidance trim and a margin dip. The bull case rests on CCB at +26%, ERP suite at +27%, and the agent roadmap converting to monetization in 2027+. The watch items: CCB deceleration into H2 as guided, whether AI productivity claims (30%) actually flatten the cost curve during the hiring freeze, and cumulative dilution from the data-layer acquisition spree (Dremio, Prior Labs, Reltio). Cost control talk was unusually concrete for SAP — headcount freeze, token chargebacks, expense-ratio commitment — suggesting management expects the AI investment phase to be self-funded rather than margin-funded.
